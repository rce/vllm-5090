# 2026-09-06 — Qwen3.8-27B under concurrency

Standard `bench.py` workload (786-token prompt, exactly 256 output tokens,
2 rounds), so these are directly comparable to the Qwen3.6-35B-A3B sweeps.

| Concurrency | default | seqs 32 | MTP + text-only |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 24.7 | 24.8 | **72.3** |
| 2 | 46.7 | 46.5 | 119.4 |
| 4 | 93.3 | 93.1 | 257.9 |
| 8 | 179.5 | 179.7 | 260.2 |
| 16 | 180.4 | 181.2 | 269.0 |
| 32 | 181.4 | **238.1** | **305.0** |

(total tok/s. `default` = `profiles/qwen3.8-27b.env` unchanged: vision on,
eager, `MAX_NUM_SEQS=8`, KV pool 61,895 tokens.)

Every level succeeded in all three configurations — 128 requests each, no
failures, no OOM.

## Per-stream decode is flat

24.9 → 23.3 tok/s from 1 to 32 concurrent streams in the default config; 75.5 →
69.5 with MTP. Batching is nearly free per caller here: an 8x load increase
costs an individual stream about 6% of its speed. That is a dense model being
weight-bandwidth-bound at NVFP4 — the weights get read once per step regardless
of how many sequences ride along.

The flat per-stream figure and the flat *total* are not in tension: per-stream
decode excludes the queueing wait, so a request that sits in the queue and then
decodes at 23 tok/s still reports 23. Total throughput is set by how many
streams actually run at once.

## MTP is the single biggest lever, and better than first measured

Three times the single-stream throughput — 24.7 → 72.3 tok/s — and it stays
ahead at every concurrency level.

**Acceptance is 0.815**, measured over 28,338 draft tokens. The 0.48 recorded in
the earlier single-stream note came from a 519-token sample and was simply too
small; this is the number to trust. The cost is unchanged: it needs
`LANGUAGE_MODEL_ONLY=1` to fit on this card, so it trades image and video input
for the speedup.

## Raising the sequence cap helps much less here than on the MoE

+31% at concurrency 32, and nothing at all below it — against +54% at
concurrency 16 and +110% at 32 for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B.

**Not changing the 27B profile**, and this time the asymmetry is the reason
rather than caution. The 27B's KV pool is 61,895 tokens against the MoE's
370,189 — vLLM reports max concurrency of 1.85x at the 27B's full 32K context.
Thirty-two concurrent long-context requests genuinely cannot fit here, where on
the MoE they could. The short-prompt gain is real but small, and it is bought
against a much thinner KV margin.

## Open question: why does the 27B plateau at ~180?

At concurrency 16 with 32 slots available and per-stream decode still at 23
tok/s, a slot-bound server should reach roughly 370 tok/s. It reaches 181. So
something other than the sequence cap is binding, and I did not chase it.

Two candidates, both untested:

- **`--enforce-eager`.** No CUDA graphs means per-step launch overhead on every
  decode step, which hurts more as the batch grows. The MoE runs with graphs on
  and scales much better — but it is also a different model, so this is not a
  controlled comparison.
- **Scheduler flags the 27B profile does not have.** The MoE profile inherited
  `--enable-chunked-prefill`, `--enable-prefix-caching` and `--async-scheduling`
  from the recipe's `rtx_5090` override. The 27B profile has none of them, so a
  786-token prefill blocks decode instead of interleaving with it.

The second is cheap to test and the more likely of the two: add chunked prefill
to the 27B profile and re-run this sweep. Worth doing before concluding anything
about dense-vs-MoE scaling from these numbers.
